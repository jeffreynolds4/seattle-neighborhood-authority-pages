Your insider guide to

South Lake Union

The city's employment engine turned neighborhood: Amazon's campus, the biotech labs, and glassy new towers wrapped around the south end of Lake Union. SLU went from warehouses to the densest new-construction condo stock in Seattle in barely fifteen years, with the streetcar, the lake, and downtown all within a few blocks.

Who thrives here: professionals who want to walk or streetcar to work, lock-and-leave condo buyers, and anyone who wants a brand-new building with the lake and the city at the door.

What to expect

Inventory here is overwhelmingly new: condos and apartments built since 2010, glass towers with amenity decks, and a handful of live-work lofts in the older brick survivors. This is a lock-and-leave market first; single-family homes are essentially absent.

Pricing spans a wide band by building, floor, and view. Newer high-floor lake-view units command a premium; interior units in the same tower are the value entry. HOA dues and amenity packages vary sharply, so the building matters as much as the unit.

Jeff's take

South Lake Union is the purest lock-and-leave play in Seattle, and it is built for a specific life: walk or streetcar to work, kayak at lunch, and never touch the car on a weekday. For Amazon and biotech buyers, nothing else is this close to the desk.

The diligence is the building, not the block. HOA reserves, dues, amenity health, and rental caps vary wildly tower to tower, and they move resale more than the view does. Reading the resale certificate first, that is the part I do.
