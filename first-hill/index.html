First Hill

Seattle's original residential hill, rising just east of downtown and long nicknamed Pill Hill for its cluster of major hospitals. Today First Hill is a dense, central condo district: pre-war apartment landmarks, mid-century towers, and the arts at the Frye, with the streetcar linking it to Capitol Hill, the International District, and light rail.

Who thrives here: condo buyers who want to be central and walkable, medical professionals near the hospitals, and lock-and-leave owners who want culture, transit, and downtown on foot.

What to expect

First Hill is a condo neighborhood almost end to end: pre-war co-ops and apartments, mid-century concrete towers, and newer glass high-rises. Detached houses are rare survivors. It is one of the most central places in Seattle to own a lock-and-leave home.

Building character varies enormously, from 1920s co-ops with rules and charm to modern towers with full amenities. HOA structures, reserves, and pet or rental rules differ sharply, so the building drives the decision as much as the view.

Only the locals know

The building is the decision

A 1920s co-op and a 2015 tower sit a block apart with wildly different rules, dues, and character. Match the building to how you live.

The Frye is a free amenity

A serious art museum with free admission, two blocks from most of the district. Locals treat it as an extension of the living room.

The streetcar earns its keep

It links the hill to Capitol Hill and, more importantly, to the light rail at the ID. In the rain it is the move.

Pre-war means character and rules

The landmark buildings are gorgeous and governed. Read the co-op rules before you fall for the terra-cotta.

Downhill is downtown

You are a walkable hill above the retail core, the ferries, and the stadiums. Central beats a car here.

Hospital blocks have a rhythm

Shift changes and ambulance routes shape a few streets. Pick your block with that in mind.

Jeff's take

First Hill is the most central lock-and-leave neighborhood in Seattle, and it is quietly overlooked. You get downtown, Capitol Hill, and light rail on foot or one streetcar stop, plus a free world-class museum and a real mix of pre-war character and modern towers.

The whole decision is the building. Co-op versus condo, reserves, pet and rental rules, and amenity health vary block to block and move resale more than the view. Reading those docs first, that is the part I do.
