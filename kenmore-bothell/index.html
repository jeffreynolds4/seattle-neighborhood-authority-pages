Your insider guide to

Kenmore Bothell

The north end of Lake Washington, where the Burke-Gilman Trail threads two growing downtowns. Kenmore sits on the water at Log Boom Park and the seaplane base; Bothell has reinvented its Main Street and its North Creek tech-and-university corridor. Together they offer some of the most attainable lake-adjacent living in the metro, with trails, breweries, and a revived town center.

Who thrives here: value-minded buyers who want lake-and-trail access, first-time and move-up families, and commuters splitting the difference between Seattle, the Eastside, and Everett.

What to expect

The mix here is broad: mid-century homes on the hills, newer townhomes and condos in and around both downtowns, and a growing stock of mixed-use near Bothell Main Street. It is one of the more attainable corners of the north Eastside, with genuine lake and trail access.

Value tracks proximity to the trail, the water, and the two revived town centers, plus commute math to Seattle, Bellevue, and Everett. Bothell's downtown reinvention has reset expectations, and newer construction sits alongside long-held family homes.

Only the locals know

The attainable lake-north

Genuine lake and trail access at a friendlier entry than most of the Eastside. Value hunters who want water nearby start here.

Bothell downtown reset the map

The Main Street reinvention, new mixed-use, and the schoolhouse redevelopment changed how the whole area trades. It is a genuine walkable core now.

The Burke-Gilman is the spine

The trail links Kenmore, Bothell, and points south by bike, all the way to the U District and Ballard. It is the north end best asset.

Three commutes, do the math

You can reach Seattle, Bellevue, or Everett from here, but each is a different drive. Pick your block around the one you make daily.

St. Edward is a hidden gem

A restored seminary, a lodge hotel, and miles of forest trail on the lake. One of the best state parks in the metro sits right here.

Northshore ties it together

Both cities share the Northshore School District and the North Creek corridor. It is more one community than the two-city name suggests.

Jeff's take

Kenmore and Bothell are where I send north-end buyers who want lake and trail access without an Eastside-waterfront price. Bothell's downtown reinvention, the Burke-Gilman, and Log Boom give you a real town-and-water life at a genuinely attainable entry.

The diligence is the trade-off map: trail and water proximity, which of the two downtowns fits you, and honest commute math to Seattle, Bellevue, and Everett. Getting that math right first, that is the part I do.
