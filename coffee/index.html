Seattle · Bryant

Cafe Blameless

Coffee favorite

An independently operated café inside the Illume church building in Bryant. The operator serves Gloom and Elm coffee, pastries and food in a gathering space open to the neighborhood.

Jeff’s take: Peaceful and serene. Sitting in this church space reminded me how special a coffee shop can be when an existing building takes on a new use.

5751 33rd Avenue NE, Seattle

Before you go: The operator currently lists a soft-opening schedule following its September 23 opening. Check the official site for current hours and event changes.

Seattle · South Lake Union

Café Hagen · South Lake Union

Coffee favorite

Café Hagen’s South Lake Union café on Thomas Street pairs specialty coffee with Scandinavian-inspired food and a focus on hygge: comfort, warmth and a slower moment in the city.

Jeff’s take: A total vibe in South Lake Union. I recently reviewed this café, and it absolutely belongs on my list.

SLU café: 1252 Thomas Street, Seattle

A few quick answers

Why does Jeff recommend Milstead & Co.?

Milstead is one of Jeff’s Seattle favorites because it is dependable and well located for exploring Fremont, nearby neighborhoods and the Ship Canal waterfront. Storyville Coffee is another longtime favorite, with about 50 visits across his experience with the brand.

Which Bellevue coffee shop does Jeff recommend?

Third Culture Coffee in Old Bellevue is Jeff’s confirmed Bellevue favorite in this edition of the guide.

Are Saint Bread and Sea Wolf coffee shops?

They belong in the bakery and café part of this guide. Jeff strongly recommends both, along with Macrina Bakery, Volunteer Park Cafe & Pantry and Oxbow.

How does this connect to Jeff’s real estate work?

Coffee and café visits are one way to explore everyday neighborhood life. For housing, building research and condo due diligence, visit Jeff’s Seattle condo specialist profile and the Bellevue condo guide. A café recommendation is not a property recommendation.
